Across the Street, version 4

Changes from the blind review

A fresh reviewer with no project context read the brief, the script, the deck and the figures, and returned thirteen findings. Twelve were applied and one was skipped. Every "after" below is what the narration or slide now says.

1

The close framed the grandchildren as the return on the loan

Video, scene 7Applied

"What you would get" is financial-win framing, and it implies family across the street depends on a yes. The one line that sounded like a pitch from the couch.

Before

This is help, not an investment. What you would get is family across the street.

After

This is help, not an investment, and they want to say that plainly. They are not offering you a return. They are asking for help with the home they hope to build, across the street from you.

2

"It is not money you lose" contradicts itself

Deck, slide 2, Amber's noteApplied

If they could earn more elsewhere, lending at 3% does cost them something. A careful parent would push back on it in the room.

Before

That gap is what a family rate closes for us. It is not money you lose, since you could earn more elsewhere either way. It is the difference between building and not building.

After

That gap is what a bank would charge us and what a family rate spares us. It is not a number you would have earned, because you are not a bank. But we know you could earn more than three percent somewhere else, and we are asking anyway, because this is the difference between building and not building.

3

Two lines presumed a yes

Deck, slide 6; video, scene 3 beat 5Applied

The brief asks for wording that makes "not yet" easy to say. "Then we build" and "soon" both read as settled.

Before

Then we build in 2027.

Papa and Grandee: cherished every week, soon nearly every day

After

If it all lines up, we hope to build in 2027.

Papa and Grandee: already every week, could be nearly every day

4

"Before anything else" came after the ask

Video, thank-you sceneApplied

Once the ask moved to scene 2 the phrase was untrue, and a thank-you right after a request reads as thanks in advance unless it is decoupled from the answer.

Before

Before anything else: thank you. For the land, and for everything you have done for this family over the years.

After

Whatever you decide about tonight, Matteo and Amber want to say thank you. For the land, and for everything you have done for this family over the years.

5

"They came with a careful plan" was followed by the dream home, not the plan

Video, scene orderApplied

The thank-you set up the plan and the film detoured through the most emotional scene first. Swapping two scenes puts the heart of the pitch right after the ask and lets "careful plan" lead straight into the plan bar.

Before

land, ask, thank you, what the home would mean, plan, numbers, done properly, let's talk

After

land, ask, what the home would mean, thank you, plan, numbers, done properly, how the IRS sees it, let's talk

6

The family-rate increase was shown but never said

Video, numbers sceneApplied

The bank-rate jump got "nearly triple" out loud; the family-rate jump was only a small line on a card. Older viewers listening on a TV deserve the number.

Before

At a family rate of around three percent, the payment would be about one thousand six hundred eighty six dollars. Still a real step up, but one they can carry.

After

At a family rate of around three percent, the payment would be about one thousand six hundred eighty six dollars. That is still about six hundred eighty six dollars a month more than they pay today. A real step up, but one they can carry.

7

The paperwork joke landed on the tax sentence, and [chuckles] laughed before the punchline

Video, scenes 3, 7 and 9Applied

Joking right after "you may owe tax on money you never receive" risked reading as flippant. And the [chuckles] tag makes the narrator laugh at his own joke before the audience hears it. All three humor lines now use a [lightly] delivery tag.

Before

...a small amount of tax might come up on your side. They would cover that, every year. [chuckles] Yes, there will be paperwork. Matteo is looking forward to it.

After

A written loan agreement, drawn up by an attorney. [lightly] Yes, there will be paperwork. Matteo is looking forward to it. [calmly] Recorded against the home, just like a bank mortgage...

8

"Attorney and CPA" appeared three times in the deck; the brief says once

Deck, appendix A2 and slide 6Applied

Removed from the sources slide and from the next-steps slide. It now appears once in the deck, on the IRS slide, and once in the film, in the IRS scene.

Before

Tax estimate assumes a 30% rate. We will confirm every detail with an attorney and CPA.

If you want to go further, we bring in an attorney and a CPA to confirm every detail.

After

Tax estimate assumes a 30% rate. Yearly gift allowance: IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32, $19,000 per giver per recipient for 2026.

If you want to go further, we bring in the professionals and come back with the paperwork.

What the reviewer said to leave alone

  • Scene 1's opening image and "close enough to walk over any time."
  • Scene 2's three cards: amount, "3% is only the example," "not a gift."
  • The plan bar: one picture, three numbers.
  • The numbers scene's shape, and "that is the number that makes the build impossible."
  • The Papa and Grandee passage: already a big part of the family's life, every week, nearly every day across the street.
  • "Possibly two" and the Lucas line. Both kept; only the delivery tag changed.
  • The ending: both houses lit, "no answer needed tonight," the hold on the card.
  • Slide 3's "we would rather you see it than wonder about it," slide 4's "we want this to feel like a real loan, because it is one," and appendix A1 in Amber's voice.

The reviewer also confirmed brief compliance on a read-through: no dashes, no "guarantee," none of the banned jargon, siblings only in the appendix, nothing about the home beyond the brief, every beat at or under 12 words, Grandee spelled consistently, and "nearly triple" accurate.
